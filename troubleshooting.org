#+TITLE: ER-420 Troubleshooting Reference
#+CATEGORY: er420
#+STARTUP: overview
#+FILETAGS: :audio:restoration:er420:

Transcribed from the service manual's own troubleshooting section
(pages 17-18). Covers the power supply and audio chain only — the
AM/FM tuner and MPX troubleshooting tables are deliberately left out,
same scope decision as the rest of this restoration.

* Power Circuit

** Does not light up or function at all

1. No power supplied.
   a. Severed power cable — check where cable enters plug.
   b. Defective power switch — check contact point.
   c. Fuse blown or no fuse inserted — replace or insert new fuse.
2. Fuse blows.
   a. Power transformer heats up — short between transformer winding
      layers.
   b. Defective insulation in OPT between primary and secondary —
      replace OPT.
   c. Short between electrodes in tubes or diodes — mainly in
      rectifier or power tube.
   d. Defective electrolytic capacitor — mainly in ripple filter
      circuit.
3. Emits smoke.
   a. Short in filament wiring — check wiring by removing tubes.
   b. Defective tube socket — mainly poor insulation of wafer-type
      socket.

** Lights up, but does not function

1. Insufficient plate voltage.
   a. Rectifier tube/diode burned out — replace tube or diode.
   b. Resistor in ripple filter circuit burned out — replace
      resistor.
   c. No rectifier.
2. No voltage at plate circuit.
   a. Primary windings of OPT burned out.

* Audio Circuit

** Does not function

1. Power amplifier section.
   a. Severed output transformer(s) windings (primary and secondary).
   b. Power output tubes damaged or heaters burned out.
   c. Plate load resistors between stages burned out.
   d. Bias resistor in cathode circuit burned out.
2. Preamplifier section.
   a. Plate load resistors between stages burned out.
   b. Poor contact(s) in input selector switch or mode switch.
   c. Defective cathode resistor.
   d. Defective de-coupling resistors between stages.
   e. Defective tubes.

** Noises

1. Tubes — defective tubes.
2. Resistors — defective plate resistors, negative feedback
   resistors, or cathode resistors.
3. Capacitors — defective capacitor in the coupling/phase
   compensation circuit.
4. Potentiometers — noise from a defective volume control or other
   variable resistor.

*** Tap test for microphonics (2026-09-14)

Tap each tube gently (insulated tool — wooden handle, never bare
metal) with volume up and no source playing, so a result isn't
masked by program material. A true *ring* — a decaying resonance
after the tap, not just the knock itself coming through — is specific
to the tube itself (loose internal elements mechanically resonating).
A socket or adjacent resistor can't produce that signature; at most
they'd crackle or pop when disturbed, not ring.

Found 2026-09-14: V3 rings clearly. See [[file:log/2026-09-13-c100-recap.org][2026-09-13 log]] for the full
tap-test results and V3's traced role (post-volume-control recovery
gain stage, both channels in one envelope).

*** Pull-tube isolation test (gadget73, AudioKarma, via Doug — 2026-09-14)

#+begin_quote
Could be noisy tubes, could be noisy resistors, could just be a noisy circuit.
Quick and dirty troubleshooting would just be to pull the first tube signal gets
to and see if it stops the noise. If not, move to the next, so on and so forth.
When you pull a tube and it goes away completely, the problem will generally be
at or immediately upstream of that stage.

Remove the tube from the socket. It will run with tubes missing but won't pass
signal. I might pull V3 and see if it shuts up. If it does, try swapping it with
another 12ax7, maybe one of the phono tubes.
--- [[https://audiokarma.org/forums/threads/new-restoration-project-pioneer-er-420.1110160/post-18483159][gadget73]]
#+end_quote

Pulling at tube will kill the signal, but there might still be audible
hum/hiss/squeal in the background. If so, that noise is being generated or
injected after the pulled tube. The inverse is also true, if pulling a tube
results in total silence the noise is at or before that pulled tube, not after
it.


** Output-stage plate/current test plan (gadget73, 2026-09-19)

For when the output tubes' plate voltages don't match each other.
Found 2026-09-19: V6 pin 7 reads 358.8V while V7/V8/V9 read ~313V —
45V apart on tubes that share a center tap (V6/V7 on T7). V6 also
read 360V on 2026-09-13, so it's consistent, not a misread.

#+begin_quote
plate voltage on output tubes is usually pretty darn close, within a
couple of volts allowing for differences in resistance of the
transformer. Might want to do an ohm check from center tap to each
plate end to confirm they match, and maybe shuffle the tubes around
to see if that voltage moves. Could be a transformer issue or could
be a tube thats not drawing anything, though normally that also means
low cathode voltage. No connection to the screen supply will also
make the tube not draw anything, wouldn't explain the cathode voltage
though.
--- gadget73, https://audiokarma.org/forums/threads/new-restoration-project-pioneer-er-420.1110160/
#+end_quote

*Why a plate reading means two things at once.* Each output tube's
plate ties to one half of its OPT primary; both halves meet at the
center tap, where B+ comes in. That winding has resistance, so:

: plate V = center-tap V − (tube current × half-primary resistance)

Two tubes on the same transformer should land within a few volts. A
*higher* plate reading means a *smaller* drop — less current through
that tube, or less resistance in its half.

*Step 0 — center-tap voltage (powered).* Never measured here. DC at
T7's and T8's center taps. This is the supply the plate readings are
measured against; it says which tube is the abnormal one rather than
just that they differ. If the CT reads ~360V, V6 is barely drawing
while V7-V9 drop ~45V.

*Step 1 — half-primary resistance (power off, caps drained).* Center
tap → pin 7 at each output socket; four readings (T7: V6, V7; T8: V8,
V9).
- Halves match (typically a few hundred ohms) :: windings are fine,
  so the voltage spread is a *current* difference.
- Halves differ a lot :: the winding explains the voltage spread and
  no tube is at fault.

*Step 2 — shuffle tubes.* Swap V6 and V7 between sockets, re-measure
both plates.
- High reading follows the tube :: that tube is the problem.
- High reading stays with the socket :: tube is fine; look at the
  socket or its wiring.
Same logic as the 2026-09-09 V1/V3 swap.

*Already ruled out here:* gadget73 lists a missing screen connection
as a cause — V6 pin 9 measured 314.9V at the pin itself, so V6's
screen supply is connected. His note that a non-drawing tube normally
also shows low cathode voltage doesn't apply cleanly to this set: all
four cathodes share one bus (see component-notes.org C600), so one
idle tube barely moves it.

*Safety:* the center tap and pin 7 carry full B+ (~360V) — the most
dangerous points in the set. Clip the black lead to chassis first,
probe one-handed, and confirm the filter caps are drained before any
resistance reading.

** Hum

1. Tubes — defective tubes, particularly a short between electrodes.
2. Resistors — defective load resistor in the phase inverter circuit,
   cathode end.
3. Capacitors — defective electrolytic capacitor in the filter
   circuit or de-coupling circuit.

** Distortion

1. Transformer — defective output transformer, output tubes, or
   rectifier (tube or diode); short between primary and secondary.
2. Capacitor — defective capacitor in the output tube bias circuit.
3. Resistor — defective grid leak resistor.
4. Rectifier — defective rectifier in the bias circuit, when a fixed
   bias circuit is employed.
