* Goal

Solder the original 2-wire cord back onto the chassis (fuse holder /
power switch), reusing the cord already in hand rather than the
still-unresolved 3-wire replacement search.

* Work

Before desoldering, photographed the existing wiring at the fuse
holder/switch for reference — this is what has to go back together
correctly once the cord's reattached.

[[file:../photos/2026-08-15-line-cord-before-desolder-01.jpg]]

While in the output-tube corner, noticed a small hardened amber
residue on the chassis floor beneath C600 — present since the very
first teardown, not new. Waxy, no smell, semi-hardened but deforms
under gentle pressure rather than cracking. That texture rules out
dried rosin flux (which ages brittle/glassy) and points instead to
either leaked/dried electrolyte or a wax end-seal compound weeping
from C600 itself, sitting directly above it. Doesn't change any
plan — C600 is already slated for replacement — but it's one more
piece of physical evidence that part has earned retirement.
Documented, not disturbed; out of the way of today's actual work at
the fuse holder.

[[file:../photos/2026-08-15-c600-area-residue-01.jpg]]
[[file:../photos/2026-08-15-c600-area-residue-02.jpg]]

Desoldered and re-terminated the original 2-wire cord.

[[file:../photos/2026-08-15-line-cord-soldered-01.jpg]]

Pulled the original glass fuse to inspect before deciding whether to
reuse it. Element looked visually intact — continuous, no blackening
of the glass — but decided to install the already-received 2A
slow-blow replacement instead rather than trust a 60-year-old
element's actual trip point from a visual check alone.

[[file:../photos/2026-08-15-fuse-old-pulled-01.jpg]]

Compared old and new side by side before installing. New fuse's
element is visibly coiled/spring-wound versus the original's straight
wire — correct visual signature of slow-blow (time-delay) construction
vs. the original's fast-blow-style element. Confirms the right part
was sourced; installed the new one — the Littelfuse 0313002.MXP (3AG
2A slo-blo), specifically, of the two fuse options in bom.org's
Miscellaneous notes.

[[file:../photos/2026-08-15-fuse-new-vs-old-01.jpg]]

Powered up on the dim bulb tester (first power-up since the cord/fuse
work) and took baseline voltage readings — full detail in
[[file:../measurements/2026-08-15-voltages.org]], summary here.
Confirmed rectifier-out, the C101/C102 doubler, both C100 sections,
and the B+ chain generally read proportionally consistent with the
schematic spec under dim-bulb suppression (~57-75% of spec across most
points). Traced C100's long-open "4th pin" mystery (from 2026-08-11)
to the screen supply, not plate as first guessed — corrected in
component-notes.org. Spent real time sorting out the 6BQ5/EL84 pin
identities on V6 after misreading the schematic's cramped print twice;
settled by an outside reference (r-type.org) rather than continued
guessing — pin 7 is plate, pin 9 is screen grid. Swept V3/V4's plate
voltages (mostly consistent with the pattern; V4 pin 6 and the
grid/cathode bias on V4's second section are outliers worth
rechecking at full B+, not necessarily faults). Swept V10-V13 in the
IF chain; light-current stages there recovered notably closer to spec
(~89-90%) than the output/preamp stages.

Amp played music cleanly throughout (aux in from Mac, speakers off at
S8) on the dim bulb tester.

* Open questions

- Hot-leg verification (Phase 5.1) was never explicitly confirmed with
  the DMM before today's power-up, despite being flagged as a
  pre-power-up step. The amp did power up and play music normally,
  which is reassuring but isn't the same as having verified the fuse
  and switch are genuinely in the hot leg — worth doing properly
  before this gets run unattended or off the bulb limiter.
- V12: no voltage readable on any pin with the auto-ranging DMM.
  Not yet determined whether the tube is seated well, whether its
  heater is lit, or whether this is a true 0V-everywhere condition —
  session ended before this could be resolved.
- V13 pin 5 and pin 6 both read 45V — not yet confirmed whether
  they're genuinely tied together or coincidentally matching.
- C600's cathode-bias reading (0.977V) and V4's second-section
  grid/cathode bias (~-21V implied) were both taken under
  dim-bulb-limited conditions and are expected to behave nonlinearly
  once measured at full B+ — neither should be treated as final until
  rechecked without the bulb.
- Still need a 3-wire cord in the gland's 2.5-6mm OD range, or a final
  decision to stay with the 2-wire original — this remains parked.
