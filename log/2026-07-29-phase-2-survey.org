* Work

Started with S7, since it moved up ahead of Phase 2/3 in the plan —
its repair gates Phase 6 (first power-up) via the Standing Rules'
"never power up with the output transformer secondary open," so it
made sense to get it moving before the rest of tonight's survey work.

Cleaned the two small metal bridge contacts and their grooves in the
plastic slider with isopropyl alcohol, then glued them back into
position with ZXH Bond B-7000 (acrylic-based, Shore A 65-80 after
cure, 3 min tack / 24-28 hr full cure). Kept glue off the contact legs
themselves — they're bent/leaf-spring shaped, not rigid, so the plan
was to bond only the flat back of each piece into its slot in the
plastic and leave the spring action free, rather than gluing the
whole piece down solid. Did the gluing fully outside the chassis on a
clean bench surface rather than in situ, since the parts were already
removed and there was no reason to risk it near other wiring.

Deliberately keeping DeOxit away from the glued joint when Phase 3's
switch treatment happens later — DeoxIT D5 is mostly odorless mineral
spirits, and mineral spirits can be harder on a solvent-based rubber
adhesive like B-7000 than on a fully-cured CA or epoxy joint. Applying
DeOxit precisely to the actual sliding contact surfaces rather than
broadly should keep the two apart.

Now curing. Per the plan: cure 24-28 hours before re-assembling the
switch and circuit board, then verify with continuity at both the 8Ω
and 16Ω positions before trusting it with anything live.

Pulled the front faceplate (all knobs and switches) to trace C96 near
S10, and found a matched pair of 0.05µF/400V oil caps in the process —
turned out to be C13/C14 (Tape Monitor path via S3), not C96. C96
itself is still unfound.

[[file:../photos/2026-07-29-c13-c14-01.jpg]]

Found C96 itself inside S10's switch housing — a green OKI-brand
0.05µF/400V cap mounted directly across the switch's own two
terminals.

[[file:../photos/2026-07-29-power-switch-02.jpg]]

First guess was that this was an undocumented arc-suppression snubber
unrelated to C96, since neither terminal reads continuity to chassis
— physically traced all three wires in the switch's gray cable bundle
(orange to the switched rear outlet via the fuse, yellow to a pin on
S9, and a separate black-insulated wire soldered directly to chassis)
and confirmed the cap connects to none of them. Re-reading the
schematic at I9-I10 more closely resolved it instead: C96 bridges
S10's contacts rather than going to chassis ground, which is exactly
what the physical trace shows. So this is C96 after all, just doing
arc-suppression duty across the switch rather than the line-to-chassis
duty originally guessed at.

Decided not to replace it. The label reads "OKI... 0.05µF (M)" with
no "OIL" marking, unlike C13/C14's explicit "OIL JCP-A" — construction
looks like resin-dipped film, not paper-in-oil (visually distinct too:
green rectangular body with straight leads, versus C13/C14's cream
cylindrical bodies with pointed metal end caps). Film caps age well,
same category as the mica/ceramic/polystyrene parts already staying
original. Duty cycle is gentle (switching transients only, not
continuous line exposure) and the failure mode if it does eventually
fail is benign (open — more arcing at the switch over time, not a
functional or safety issue) — the one failure mode that would matter,
shorted, would need checking for but hasn't shown up. Phase 2's
"Trace C96" item closed.

Traced R77/R79/R80 (and R78/R80's right-channel mirror) while working
the same area — found they tap in before S8 rather than after,
answering the standing question about whether "speakers off" recreates
a no-load condition on T7/T8. Full writeup in circuit-notes.org; short
version is it doesn't, as long as R77-R80 stay intact. R79/R80 turned
out to be 16Ω on the actual part, not the 10Ω the manual's parts list
states — trusted the physical marking, logged in bom.org.

Identified C49 (V6's grid coupling cap) by cross-checking three things
at once: tube position, the EL84's known pin-2-is-grid pinout, and the
adjacent resistor reading 2.2K — an exact match to R73. Found three
more caps of the same "A.1-M 400V" construction clustered near the
other output tubes, consistent with C49-C52 all being physically
grouped together. Ran an in-circuit DC leakage check (DMM resistance
mode) on all four — all read clean, no leakage detected, no legs
lifted since the results weren't ambiguous enough to need it.

[[file:../photos/2026-07-29-c13-c14-01.jpg]]

Got some secondhand advice midway through claiming these are Nissei
"Type A" capacitors with a decodable date-code marking, and separately
that 1960s molded caps like this have a "near-100% failure rate" and
should be replaced automatically without testing. Checked both claims
before acting on them: Nissei Electric is a real film capacitor
manufacturer, but nothing about a "Type A" series or that specific
marking scheme turned up anywhere, and the "replace without testing"
advice directly contradicts both the Reddit thread read earlier
tonight and the clean test results already in hand. Didn't act on
either claim. Phase 2's C49-C52 test item closed.

Checked bom.org for other 0.1µF/400V Mylars beyond C49-C52 — found
four more, C9/C10/C21/C22, same spec exactly. Ran the same in-circuit
leakage check on all four: OL or 1MΩ+ on every one, no leakage. That's
all eight of the amp's 0.1µF Mylars now tested clean. Phase 2's
remaining-Mylars item closed.
