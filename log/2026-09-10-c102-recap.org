* Goal

Short session, planned as just pulling C102 (same recap candidate as
C101, same single-section Nichicon construction) — ended up completing
the full re-stuff in one sitting.

* Work

Discharge-checked C101/C102 fresh — 0V across both to chassis, clear
to proceed.

Photographed the original wiring before touching anything: black and
brown wires twisted together onto the positive terminal, a bare ground
wire and a diode onto the negative terminal. A nearby blue wire passes
through this area but isn't part of C102's circuit.

[[file:../photos/2026-09-10-c102-recap-01.jpg]]

Desoldered the wires off the terminals — messier than C101's job, the
black and brown wires' insulation melted back a bit from the heat.
Dremeled the can open, pulled the old element with pliers, cleaned the
inside of the can.

[[file:../photos/2026-09-10-c102-recap-02.jpg]]

Same method as C101: drilled two holes next to the terminal header's
soldering posts, ran the new Nichicon 100µF/350V cap's leads through,
matched negative to the black-painted terminal, crimped to the posts.
Before soldering the black/brown pair back onto the new cap's positive
lead, sleeved them to cover the section where insulation had melted
back. Reassembled into the can, reinstalled in the chassis in the
original orientation, fed the original wires back through the terminal
holes, soldered.

[[file:../photos/2026-09-10-c102-recap-03.jpg]]

Pre-power checks: no continuity between either new terminal and the
chassis/can shell, or between the two terminals. Clear to power up.

Power on, dim bulb tester as usual. A few minutes' warm-up — enough to
trust this reading, since the B+/doubler rail isn't subject to the slow
tube-cathode warm-up curve that mattered for V1 Pin 3 on 2026-09-08/09.
73.3VAC input, C101+ 214.5V, C101-/C102+ midpoint 106.7V (both sides
agree), C102- 0V (correctly grounded). Doubler symmetry (106.7V vs.
half of 214.5V = 107.25V, a 0.4977 ratio) matches C101's own
post-replacement check from last night. Both halves of the doubler are
now new and reading as a healthy matched pair.
