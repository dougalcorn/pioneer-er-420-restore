Try the silver-paint repair on VR2's ground terminals (from

* Work

** Silver-paint attempt on VR2 — did not work, left it slightly worse

Yuecoom silver conductive paste arrived (ordered 2026-09-18). Applied
to *both* gangs, in place, pot still wired in: the carbon film has a
lip standing proud of the metal ground tab, so the paste went down
that lip as a stripe on both sides of the tab, applied with the
syringe's needle. Messier than intended — harder to control than it
looked, and some paste ended up on the phenolic wafer, scraped off
with the needle after it dried. No scrap test was run first.

Cure: hair dryer ~5 minutes, then left ~4 hours.

Result by ear: still significant sound at minimum volume, no obvious
improvement.

Measurements after (power off):
- Paint → chassis :: <1Ω. So the paste cured, conducts, and is bonded
  to the (grounded) tab. The paste itself is not the problem.
- Needle on carbon film just in front of the tab :: ~28kΩ.
- Needle on the film right at the wafer edge, where it's sandwiched
  under the tab :: ~18kΩ.
- Wiper → ground at minimum :: *22kΩ*, against 14.85k on 2026-09-18.
  Worse, not better — higher resistance at minimum means *more*
  signal gets through.

** What that says, including a correction

The film readings can't be taken at face value: 18k at the tab is
*higher* than the 14.85k the wiper read, and the wiper sits further
along the same film. Several kΩ of every needle-on-carbon reading is
contact resistance between a steel point and a carbon film, not the
pot.

That undermines 2026-09-18's diagnosis. The 15.49k "ground lug →
film at the clamp" reading was read as proof that the tab-to-film
clamp had gone resistive; it was probably mostly probe contact
resistance. Claude drew that conclusion and recommended the paint on
the strength of it.

Most likely reading now: the resistance is spread along the film
between the grounded end and wherever the wiper actually rests — i.e.
the wiper does stop short in resistance terms, even though it looks
aligned with the terminal through the case window. Bridging at the
tab parallels a few mm of film that were never the problem, which is
why nothing improved.

Why 14.85k → 22k is unknown: paste that crept past the film's edge
onto the wiper's path, or damage from scraping the dried paste off,
are the two candidates. Both are on the inner face, invisible from
outside.

** Voltage sweep

Full mains (120VAC), no variac, no dim bulb, Fluke 15B+, chassis
reference, selector on AUX, volume down, ~10+ min warm-up. Readings
dictated in voice mode. Full table in
[[file:../measurements/2026-09-19-voltages.org][2026-09-19 measurements]].

Settling since 2026-09-13 changed nothing: every row is within ~1% of
that session, except V3 (new JJ ECC803s, so a different tube). The
09-13 numbers were valid.

* Measurements

See [[file:../measurements/2026-09-19-voltages.org][measurements/2026-09-19-voltages.org]]. Three findings worth calling
out:

1. *V4 pin 6 is not a fault — the schematic's 85V label is wrong.*
   V5 (right-channel mirror, never measured before) reads pin 6
   197.9V / pin 8 43.4V against V4's 197.6V / 45.3V. Both channels
   agree, both cathodes on spec. This was gadget73's own cross-check.
2. *The "(K)" markings at VR5/VR6 are explained.* V1 pin 4 (a heater
   pin) reads 19.49V DC — the same as the cathode bus — while V8's
   heater pin reads 0V. V1/V2's heater winding is elevated to the
   output-stage cathode bus, a standard hum-reduction move for the
   most sensitive preamp stage. Open in circuit-notes.org since July.
3. *The output tubes' plates don't match.* V6 pin 7 reads 358.8V
   (+7.1%, and 360V on 09-13 — consistent, not a misread) while V7,
   V8, V9 read ~313V (-6.4%). Everything else in the set runs ~+5%, so
   V6 fits the pattern and V7-V9 don't. V6/V7 are the push-pull pair
   on T7 and should match. This is the live lead on gadget73's
   excessive-current concern.

Cathode bus (row 11) read 19.53V then 19.7V, matching 09-13's 19.77V.

* Open questions

- Per-tube output current, still unmeasured — the way to get it
  without the cathode-bus confusion: powered, DC at each output
  transformer's center tap (T7, T8); power off, resistance from each
  center tap to each plate (pin 7). Drop ÷ resistance = that tube's
  current. Answers gadget73 tube by tube.
- Why V6's plate sits ~45V above V7-V9's.
- VR2: why the wiper reading went 14.85k → 22k after the paint.
  Invisible from outside.
- The 26Ω cathode-bus-to-chassis reading (2026-09-19, cold) is still
  unexplained — heater elevation was ruled out for V8 (pin 4 = 0V
  DC), and 19.7V ÷ 26Ω would be impossible current. Suspect the
  ohmmeter was reading C600 charging rather than a settled value;
  re-measure with the probes held until it stops climbing.

* Next

- VR2: leaning toward desoldering it and disassembling it on the
  bench — not tonight. Every path except living with the bleed starts
  with the same desolder, so buying a replacement first only saves
  downtime. Prep is unchanged from 2026-09-18: lead map first,
  tape-flag every wire, don't cut leads, bench baseline (total, wiper
  minimum, loudness tap out-of-circuit, mid-rotation for taper, plus
  shaft/bushing dimensions) before opening anything.
- Replacement options if the repair fails:
  - *New remanufactured pots*: eBay seller =hifiaudio= (22k feedback,
    100% positive) sells new loudness-tap volume pots per Pioneer
    model, $90 + shipping. Era-matched candidates are the *SX-1000TA
    / TD / TW* (mid-'60s tube receivers); the rest of their range
    (SX-950, SX-850, SA-900, SX-6000, SA-6500, SX-700T) is '70s
    solid-state and likely 50k/100k, which would load the stage ahead
    of VR2 and change the tone. Ask the seller: resistance/taper, tap
    position, shaft and bushing dimensions, and whether they can build
    to ER-420/LX-420A spec. Needs our own bench numbers first.
  - *Donor units* (all inference, nothing confirmed): the AR8542
    number appears nowhere online — '60s Pioneer manuals are
    image-only scans, so parts lists aren't searchable. AR numbers
    identify the part, not the model (VR3/VR4 already share AR8546),
    and cross-model sharing is proven for the ER-420's AT6123U output
    transformer (also in SX-82, SM-83). Best donor bets: *LX-420A*
    (hifi-wiki calls the ER-420 an "LX-420A with LW" — same tube count
    and power class), then SX-34B/LX-34B and SX-410 (both 1966), then
    SX-800/LX-800, SX-82, SM-83, SA-400. Any donor needs a Loudness
    Contour switch. Certainty would need a parts-list page from an
    LX-420A or SX-34B manual scan.
- Output-stage current measurement above, before any long listening
  sessions.
- Remaining sweep rows: 4, 41-42 (R67/R68 supply end), and the FM/AM
  rows (21-34, 18a/18b/19a/19b).
