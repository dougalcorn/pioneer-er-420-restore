* Work

Before opening back up: verified 0V across chassis and all pins of
C99-C102 with the DMM.

Confirmed the swap pair as V1/V3 (not V2), matching the 2026-09-08 plan —
both are 12AX7(T) per bom.org, a valid like-for-like swap.

Pulled the metal shields off V1 and V3 to access the tubes; left off for
this test since it's a static DC measurement, not a hum/listening check
— shields will go back on before any signal test. V3's socket was
noticeably tighter than V1's on both the pull and the reseat; V1's
socket felt like normal resistance both removing the original tube and
inserting V3's, which argues against a loose V1 socket contact as the
explanation for Pin 3's 0V reading.

Moved V3's tube into V1's socket. S1 left on AUX, matching the mode
2026-09-08's baseline V1 Pin 3 reading was taken under.

Power on at 5:44pm. Dim bulb settled to a stable glow in under a minute.
5:50pm: 73.4VAC measured across the receiver's own line input — close to
2026-09-08's 71.6V, so tonight's conditions should be comparable to that
session's.

5:52pm: V1 Pin 3 read 0V, but this is only ~8 minutes into warm-up —
per 2026-09-08's finding that the first-pass V1 readings were cold-tube
artifacts (superseded after 20+ minutes), too early to treat as
conclusive.

6:15pm (~31 minutes in, past the 20-minute mark that mattered last
session): V1 Pin 3 still reads 0V.

While waiting out the warm-up window, physically identified and
photographed R101 — the horizontal printed-text resistor near C99 with
the blue wire running underneath it, part of the KOA-branded cluster
2026-09-08 flagged as printed-text rather than color-banded. Added a
dedicated [[file:../component-notes.org::*R101][R101]] entry to

Ran step 2 of the swap: moved V1's original tube into V3's socket (V3's
tube stayed in V1's socket from step 1 — full cross-swap, not a
three-way). Same power cycle, so warm-up was already well past 20
minutes; no fresh cold-tube risk for this reading.

Result: V3 Pin 3 read 0V (dead) and V3 Pin 1 read 149.5V — elevated

Powered off, swapped both tubes back to their original sockets

Powered off at the end of this test sequence.

** C101 recap

Discharge-checked C101/C102 fresh (power had been cycled several times
by this point) — 0.2V across C101+ to chassis, well under any hazard
threshold, clear to proceed.

Decision on approach: re-stuff the original Nichicon can rather than
mount the new part on a separate terminal strip, after reviewing an
AudioKarma restoration thread on this same approach. Confirmed C101 is
a single-section cap (unlike the multi-tap C99/C100 cans), so no
shared-ground complexity to sort out.

Disassembly: unclamped and pulled the can. Photographed the original
wiring (two wires, one per terminal, straightforward joint — no shared
junctions) before touching anything.

[[file:../photos/2026-09-09-c101-recap-01.jpg]]

Dremeled the outer aluminum sleeve open to see the construction: an
aluminum-can outer sleeve over a paper-wrapped capacitor element, with
a separate two-lug terminal header (orange/brown sealant, two rigid
posts) crimped into one end. Black material at the element's cut ends
crumbled into brittle chips rather than fraying like burnt paper —
reads as normal pitch/wax end-seal compound, not heat damage.

[[file:../photos/2026-09-09-c101-recap-02.jpg]]

Element was printed "nichicon ELECTROLYTIC CAPACITOR 100MFD 250WV
NEGATIVE BLACK, Nichicon Capacitor Ltd." — matches bom.org's listed
original spec exactly, so this reads as the original part, not a prior
repair's replacement.

[[file:../photos/2026-09-09-c101-recap-03.jpg]]

Rejected two other approaches before settling on the final method:
mounting the new cap loose on a separate terminal strip (would work
but loses the original look, and the empty can would just be
decorative rather than functional); and soldering the new leads
directly to the terminal header's flimsy folded tabs then epoxying the
can shut with the new cap potted inside (rejected — those tabs have no
mechanical support once separated from the crimped shell, and potting
a modern electrolytic in epoxy blocks its vent, turning a normal aging
failure into a worse one with nowhere to vent).

Final method: drilled two holes through the terminal header to reach
the rigid posts underneath (sturdier anchor point than the folded
tabs), ran the new Nichicon 100µF/350V cap's leads through the holes,
and test-fit the crimp before soldering.

[[file:../photos/2026-09-09-c101-recap-04.jpg]]

Polarity confirmed by matching the new cap's striped (negative) lead
to the same post the old element's black-marked negative lead was on
— per component-notes.org this post is C101's "−", tied to C102's "+"
at the doubler midpoint, not to chassis ground. Soldered both leads to
the posts.

[[file:../photos/2026-09-09-c101-recap-05.jpg]]

Added electrical tape inside the can to keep the new cap's body from
touching the shell. Drilled a vent hole in the can's top, though in
hindsight the lead-holes in the terminal header would likely have
vented enough on their own. Left the can air-filled, no potting.
Reassembled can to header using the original clamp only (no adhesive
available) — confirmed a snug fit, took some trial and error to get
the terminals realigned to their original rotational position.
Resoldered the original two wires to the same terminals, marking the
negative lead's insulator edge with pen before final assembly so
polarity stays checkable without opening it back up.

[[file:../photos/2026-09-09-c101-recap-06.jpg]]

Pre-power checks: no continuity between either new terminal and the
chassis, the can shell, or each other. Clear to power up.

Power on 9:16pm, dim bulb tester as usual. 9:23pm (~7 min in): 73.4VAC
input, C101+ 212.5V, C101−/C102+ midpoint 106V (both sides agree),
C102− 0V (correctly grounded, matches component-notes.org). C101+'s
212.5V against 2026-09-08's 209.4V baseline tracks almost exactly with
tonight's slightly higher line voltage (73.4V vs. 71.6V) — consistent,
not a red flag. Doubler symmetry (106V vs. half of 212.5V = 106.25V) is
tighter than 2026-09-08's already-good split. Reads as a clean,
successful replacement.

Full cross-swap result, laid out as a table:

| Tube               | Socket                          | Pin 3 (cathode)                      |
|--------------------+---------------------------------+--------------------------------------|
| V3's original tube | V3's own (2026-09-08, pre-swap) | 0.322V — low but alive               |

| V1's original tube | V3's socket                     | 0V — dead                            |

Reads as two separate, coincident faults rather than one clean
explanation:
- V1's socket/circuit kills cathode current for *any* tube put in it
  (V3's known-good tube went dead there too) — points at V1's
  socket/circuit itself, independent of the tube.
- V1's original tube has never conducted in either socket tested
  (including V3's otherwise-good one) — points at the tube's section 1
  being genuinely bad, separately from the socket issue.

Not resolved tonight; deliberately set aside to move to the C101
replacement. Next session should pick this back up — likely a third
known-good 12AX7(T) in V1's socket (if one's on hand) to confirm the
socket fault independent of both existing tubes, and/or tracing V1's
socket contacts and grid circuit at Pin 2 directly.
