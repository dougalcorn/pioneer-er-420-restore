** Pull-tube isolation test — procedure

Signal path, per the AudioKarma post and the voltage tables: V1 (left)
| V2 (right) first gain → volume (VR2) → V3 (both channels, recovery
gain) → V4 | V5 (per channel: voltage amp + split-load phase inverter)
→ V6-V9 outputs. V1/V2 and V4/V5 are parallel pairs, not series — so
every step is judged per channel.

Setup:
- S1 on AUX, AUX inputs shorted (RCA plugs, center tied to shell) —
  an open jack picks up hum/RF of its own.
- Balance centered. Speakers physically separated; judge each by ear
  up close so the other channel doesn't bleed in.
- One fixed volume mark, written down, same every step.
- Judgment by ear: gone / better / same / worse. If a change is
  marginal, reinsert and re-listen to the baseline back-to-back.

Each step:
1. Volume down, power off. Let glass cool or use a glove; twist off
   the shield (V1-V3).
2. Pull one tube only. Label every tube with its socket.
3. Power on, wait 30-60 s for warm-up until the noise stops
   changing, then bring volume to the mark and listen, L and R.
4. Volume down, power off, reinsert before the next step.

Order:
0. Baseline, all tubes in.
0b. Volume sweep, all tubes in — min to mark. Noise that rises with
    volume enters upstream of VR2 (V1/V2, input); noise that doesn't
    change enters downstream (V3 onward).
1. V1 pulled (left first gain).
2. V2 pulled (right first gain).
3. V3 pulled (both channels).
4. V4 pulled / 5. V5 pulled — *gated*: only if noise remains with V3
   pulled, and only after checking the supply rail feeding V4/V5 and
   the coupling-cap voltage rating on that plate. V4 pin 6 already
   reads 197.6V vs 85V spec (2026-09-13); pulling V4 unloads that
   node toward its rail.
- V6-V9 not pulled.

Reading results: noise remaining with tube N pulled is generated
downstream of N. Where it drops, the source is in that stage — the
tube, its plate/cathode resistors, coupling cap, or socket. A drop in
steps rather than all at once means more than one source.

Pulled V3's shield with the set on and program playing, tapped the
bare glass with the wooden paintbrush handle: no ringing. Compare
2026-09-13, where the ring was heard tapping *through the shield*
with music paused. Not yet an apples-to-apples retest (music was

** Volume at minimum still passes music

Both AUX and TAPE MON: music audible with VR2 turned all the way
down. Level, channel(s), and tonal character (full-range vs. thin/
treble-only) not yet noted.

Characterized (turntable on AUX): full-spectrum and loud — about a
comfortable background-music level with VR2 at minimum. Not
capacitive crosstalk; a real signal path.

Why that's odd, per tile-C5-E7: VR1 (balance) wiper → VR2 top; VR2
wiper → C21 (0.1µF) → V3 grid; VR2 bottom → the common ground bus
(which also carries VR1's center, the R33/R34 grid leaks, and S4 1/2's
ground). 2026-08-04 DMM: VR2 wiper reaches ~20Ω to ground at minimum,
ground lug 0Ω to chassis (power off). If that holds live, nothing
coming through VR2 can reach V3 at minimum — so the signal is either
bypassing VR2 or the ground bus isn't really at ground under power.
One bypass the schematic offers: the loudness network (VR2's fixed
tap, which carries signal regardless of wiper position → S4 → C19 →
R31 15k → ground bus). If R31's far end landed on V3's grid side
instead of the bus (miswire, or a prior repair), loudness would feed
the grid directly at any volume setting. Unconfirmed.

Test: loudness (S4) on vs. off at minimum volume — no difference.
S4 swaps the tap's series cap between C17 (100p) and C19 (0.02µF),
so if the loudness path fed the grid, the level would change sharply
between positions. Loudness path ruled out.

loud" at pickup — but that was with S7 physically broken (popping,
garbled channels), and every session until 2026-09-13 ran on the dim

Test: V3 pulled, volume at minimum, turntable playing — no sound at
all. The bleed enters at or before V3: V3's grid side (C21/C22,
grid wiring, R33/R34) or the ground bus VR2 and the grid leaks share
— not downstream at V4/V5.

Test: balance (VR1) swept, V3 back in, volume at minimum, turntable
playing.
- Balance full left :: left bleeds, and *crackly*; right still has
  sound but much quieter.
- Balance full right :: right bleeds, clear; left silent.

Both channels bleed. The bleed follows the balance control, so it
travels the normal path — through VR1, into VR2's top — and VR2 isn't
shunting it to ground at minimum. Points at VR2 itself (wiper not
reaching its ground end live) or VR2's ground end / the shared ground
bus not truly at ground. The crackle on the left channel is an
intermittent contact in that channel's VR1 or VR2 gang — plausibly
the same source as the 2026-09-13 scratchy noise. The asymmetry
(right not fully cut at full-left balance, left fully cut at
full-right) not yet explained.

In-place resistance, power off, volume at minimum, balance centered
(before unmounting VR2). VR2 has a front and a rear set of lugs;
which gang is which channel not yet known.
- VR2 rear, wiper (12:00) → ground lug (10:00) :: *15kΩ* (expect
  ~20Ω). In-circuit parallel paths can only pull a reading *down*, so
  the real wiper-to-ground-end resistance is at least 15k. Against a
  500k element that's roughly 30 dB of attenuation at "zero" — about
  the background-music level heard. Likely the bleed, at least for
  this gang.
- VR2 front and rear, ground lug (10:00) → chassis :: 0Ω both.
  External ground path is fine; the 15k is inside the rear gang
  (wiper-to-track-end contact, or the ground-end termination rivet).
- VR2 front, wiper → chassis (= ground lug, per the 0Ω above) ::
  *10.67kΩ*. Both gangs high at minimum — consistent with both
  channels bleeding. Compare 2026-08-04: one gang read ~20Ω at
  minimum shortly after the 2026-07-28 DeoxIT pass (the other gang's
  minimum wasn't recorded). If that reading was genuinely at minimum,
  the wiper contact has regressed since.
- VR2 rear, rocking test at the CCW stop (meter on wiper → ground) ::
  resting 14.95k; pressed into stop → brief fluctuation, settles
  14.87k; backed off a hair CW → 18.79k; back to stop → 14.84k;
  again: stop 14.88k, off 16.29k, stop 14.85k. Very repeatable at the
  stop (~14.85k), no erratic jumps — not a dirty/intermittent contact.
  The wiper reaches a consistent point that is still ~15k up from the
  ground lug: either the ground-end termination (rivet/pad) has gone
  resistive, or the wiper's travel stops short of it. Can't tell
  those apart from outside the pot.
- VR2 front, rocking test at the CCW stop :: 10.67k → off 17.78k →
  stop 10.68k → off 17.6k → stop 10.69k. Same behavior as the rear:
  steady, repeatable, not a dirty contact. Both gangs stop ~10-15k
  short of ground. Both failing the same way suggests a shared cause
  (the common shaft/stop, or age-worn end terminations on both tracks)
  rather than one broken rivet.

Confirmed by sweeping balance, same measurement:

- Balance full left :: front 398k, rear 136k.
The low reading swaps sides with the balance knob, so it's VR1's
parallel path, not the VR2 tracks. It also maps the gangs: balance
full right pulls the *left* channel's VR1 wiper toward ground, and
the front reading dropped — so *front gang = left channel, rear gang
= right channel*. That makes the rear (15k at minimum, the worse
bleed) the right channel, and the crackle heard on the left (at
full-left balance) the front gang's or VR1-left's.

Decision: *not* desoldering VR2. Pulling it would mean redoing ~8
original, non-faulty joints and taking the set out of service until
the pot is repaired; it's usable as-is, just without a true zero.
Repair deferred until a donor pot (used AR8542 or a matching A-500KΩ
dual with loudness tap) is in hand, so the swap is one evening and
the original can be repaired on the bench without a deadline.

Considered and set aside: a dot of silver conductive paint applied
from outside the case. The fault is on the inside face of the wafer
(rivet-to-track, or the gap between the wiper's stop and the
ground-end pad); everything reachable outside is already solid metal
to chassis (0Ω). Blind application risks shorting part of the track
or bridging to the tap/input lugs. It *is* a legitimate repair from
the inside — extending the ground-end pad to where the wiper stops —
if the rear section is ever opened (one joint: the shared ground).
Worth having the paint on hand for that.

VR2 remounted (flat washer + hex nut, as removed). Listen check with
turntable on AUX — volume sweep, minimum, balance extremes, normal
listening — all back to the same behavior as before removal.

Confirming test, volume at minimum, set playing: shorting both VR2
wipers to chassis → completely silent. Shorting one wiper silences
one speaker; the other wiper, the other speaker. The whole bleed
comes through VR2's wiper-to-ground residual, per channel; nothing
downstream (C21/C22, V3 grid wiring) adds a path of its own.
Diagnosis confirmed.

Looking through the case windows with the shaft a hair shy of the
CCW stop: the wiper's contact dimple is visible just short of the
ground terminal and appears aligned with it — Doug's read is that
the wiper is *not* stopping short. Each section shows the terminal
sitting on top of a black layer over a brown layer: black = the
carbon film, brown = the phenolic substrate. If the wiper does reach
the terminal, the 10-15k is in the terminal-to-carbon contact (the
lug/rivet clamping onto the film) rather than wiper travel — which
also puts that joint at the edge, reachable from outside. Not yet
confirmed by measurement:

Needle-probe test, power off: ground lug → carbon film immediately
beside the terminal's clamp, rear (right) section: *15.49kΩ* —
essentially the same as the 14.85k wiper-at-minimum reading. So the
~15k sits between the lug and the film right at the clamp: the
terminal-to-carbon contact has gone resistive. Not wiper travel, not
track wear. That joint is at the wafer edge and reachable from
outside — a targeted silver-paint bridge (terminal edge onto the
film, clear of the wiper path) is now a real candidate repair. Front
section not yet probed.

** S3 crosstalk: unselected AUX bleeds into TAPE MON

TAPE MON on (S3 selecting the TAPE MON jack), Mac paused on TAPE MON,
Mani 2 + turntable playing on AUX: with volume turned up, the
turntable is audible. So the unselected source leaks past S3. Likely
the same family as the volume-at-minimum bleed above: signal getting
around a selector or level control rather than through it.

Follow-up: the leak is treble-only, no audible bass, and only heard
with volume quite high. With volume at minimum (TAPE MON on, Mac
paused) the turntable is inaudible. Treble-only = capacitive coupling
across S3's contacts/wiring (reactance falls with frequency) — normal
for a vintage selector, not a fault. And since it disappears at
minimum volume, it enters upstream of VR2: a separate issue from the
volume-at-minimum bleed, not the same mechanism.
