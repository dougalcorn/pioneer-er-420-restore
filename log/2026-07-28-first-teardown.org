#+TITLE: First teardown — 2026-07-28
#+DATE: 2026-07-28
#+FILETAGS: :audio:restoration:er420:

* Goal

Remove the bottom cover, discharge and verify every capacitor
section, and photograph the underside before anything gets touched.

* Work

Chronological. Reference photos inline where they happen; the
surrounding sentence is the caption.

Top case screws are flathead (2 per side), not JIS as the plan
assumed — corrected in restoration-plan.org. All of them have factory
paint-dot seals intact, never removed before.

With the top off: all tubes visible, plus several of the big
electrolytic cap cans.

[[file:../photos/2026-07-28-top-cover-off-01.jpg]]

Both output transformers (T7/T8, AT6123U) clearly visible in the
overview shot — nothing obviously charred or bulging, but that's
eyeballing, not a real check. Still want continuity/insulation
checked given the possible no-load event from acquisition night.

Photographed the back panel too. Located S7 and S9 by matching the
rear legend plate against the physical switches:

[[file:../photos/2026-07-28-back-panel-01.jpg]]
[[file:../photos/2026-07-28-back-panel-02.jpg]]

S9 (line voltage) is the switch tagged "115V," far left of the
panel, right below the LINE VOLTAGE legend icon. S7 (speaker
impedance) is the small slide switch next to the black 4-terminal
block positioned near MPX SEPARATION, matching the legend's SPKR IMP
icon. Photo-based match, not electrically traced yet. Updated
bom.org.

Discharged every capacitor section before going further. All read
dead — no residual charge anywhere.

While doing that, pinned down C99 and C100 physically: each is a
3-section can with 4 terminals (one common + one per section), not
the single node I'd assumed. Confirmed which terminal is common with
a meter rather than guessing from wire color — black-wire lug on both
cans reads continuity to chassis, matching a nearby tie-point strip's
grounded center lug. Full writeup in [[file:../schematic/circuit-notes.org][circuit-notes.org]] under Power
supply, including how the schematic shows these sections cascading
down the B+ filter chain rather than all tapping one node.

Also found the etched label on C99's own can — "ELYT.CAP. W. 40-20-20µF
350V," pins numbered 1-4 with pin 4 marked NEG — matching the meter
reading exactly, straight from the factory stamp:

[[file:../photos/2026-07-28-c99-label-01.jpg]]

C101/C102 (the voltage-doubler caps) physically measured too: can is
67mm tall × 25mm dia, versus 71mm × 25mm for C99/C100. Confirmed which
is which by tracing D3/D4 physically — see circuit-notes.org — and
after an initial mix-up (logged, then corrected the same night), C102
is the rear-panel-closest can, matching the manual's own layout
diagram after all.

Checked T7/T8 for no-load damage (S8 may have been left off while
powered at acquisition — see [[file:../history.org][history.org]]). Primary halves matched well
on both (T8: 122/122Ω, T7: 127/128Ω — no shorted turns), and
primary-to-secondary showed a slow climb rather than a low stable
reading on both, which is what arc damage would look like. Full
writeup and caveats in circuit-notes.org; real confirmation still
waits for Phase 5 first power-up.

Spent a long stretch tracing T9 (the power transformer) and the
130V/doubler circuit around D3, D4, C101, and C102 — wire colors,
which side of T9 the primary vs. secondary leads exit from, and how
D3/D4 relate to the two caps. Got the D3/D4 identification backwards
at first (thought the diode touching the grounded cap terminal was
D3; it's actually D4, per the schematic), corrected once the physical
tracing was clear enough to be sure. Also traced V1/V2's hum-balance
setup (VR5/VR6) and realized they're heater-related, not
cathode-related, despite a "(K)" symbol on the schematic that reads
like it should mean cathode — checked V1/V2's actual cathode circuits
directly and they don't have a matching symbol, so this schematic
apparently uses "(K)" for two different things in two different
places. Full detail, all corrections included rather than just the
final answer, in circuit-notes.org under Power supply.

Found a probable running-production addition while looking at V8: a
factory-wired heater hum-balance resistor (wire-wound, ends on V8
pins 4/5, grounded center tap) that doesn't appear on the schematic
despite checking the tiles that should show it. Wiring is dressed
under other factory wiring, so it reads as original, not a field
repair.

Also confirmed C94/C95 physically — the two AC-line safety caps near
the fuse holder and T9's primary, matched on both value (0.002µF) and
position, with a caveat that the manual's 1400V spec and the printed
150VAC marking are probably two different rating conventions rather
than a contradiction:

[[file:../photos/2026-07-28-fuse-holder-c94-c95-01.jpg]]

And a good originality find on S9: the locking plate that pins it to
115V isn't a field addition — the back of the plate reads 230V, and
the chassis shows uniform patina around it with a shiny protected
patch underneath, consistent with it being mounted since
near-manufacture and just flipped to the correct face for the US
export market.

Diagnosed S7 (the broken rear impedance selector): mechanical, not
electrical. All parts are present and the wiring/solder joints look
intact — the two small metal bridge contacts have simply come
unglued from their grooves in the plastic slider.

[[file:../photos/2026-07-29-s7-teardown-01.jpg]]
[[file:../photos/2026-07-29-s7-teardown-02.jpg]]

Looks repairable by re-gluing the contacts back into position rather
than needing replacement or a hardwire-to-16Ω workaround — changes
the framing of Phase 7's "repair vs. hardwire" question. Worth a
dry-fit through the pin comb to confirm contact at both the 8Ω and
16Ω positions before committing any adhesive, and keeping glue off
the actual contact faces so the repair doesn't just trade a broken
switch for a high-resistance one.

Across the night, closed out Phase 0 down to just the dim bulb tester
[3/4], all of Phase 1 [5/5], and Phase 2 up to [8/15] — see
restoration-plan.org for the full breakdown of what's done.

* Measurements

- T8: primary brown-red 122Ω, brown-green 122Ω
- T7: primary brown-red 127Ω, brown-green 128Ω
- Both: primary-to-secondary climbed slowly toward roughly 21kΩ
  rather than settling, in-circuit (not lifted out) — see
  circuit-notes.org for why that's expected and not a red flag
- C99/C100 can: 71mm tall × 25mm dia
- C101/C102 can: 67mm tall × 25mm dia

* Open questions

- Secondary winding lead grouping on T9 doesn't obviously reconcile —
  6 physical wires against roughly 5 secondary winding functions on
  the schematic. Likely a shared common return; not confirmed by
  resistance grouping yet.
- D3's exact physical terminal (on C101) traced from the schematic but
  not independently confirmed the way D4's was.
- V8's heater-balance resistor still has no schematic designator.
