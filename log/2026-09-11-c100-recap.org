* Goal

Continue the power-supply recap: C100 next (multi-section, 40+20+20µF
— a harder job than C101/C102's single-section cans, shared-ground
handling applies). Discrete replacement approach per
er-420-restoration-plan.org Phase 5 item 4: 47µF + 22µF + 22µF per can.

Before opening up tonight: confirmed the can's pinout against its own
printed label and physical wire colors (see component-notes.org's C100
entry) — pin 1 (brown wire) = 40µF section, pin 2 and pin 3 (both
orange, tagged before desoldering to avoid mixing them up) = the two
20µF sections, pin 4 (two black wires) = negative/common, physically
at the bottom of the can with pin 2 centered above it, pin 1 left,
pin 3 right.

* Work

Side thread before touching the chassis: chased down what turned out
to be the TAPE REC/P.B DIN jack, prompted by wanting a second
line-level input. Full trace is in
[[file:../schematic/circuit-notes.org::*S3 (Tape Monitor ON/OFF) topology, read 2026-09-11][circuit-notes.org]]
— corrected a wrong 2026-08-05 assumption (TAPE MONITOR is not on
S1's wafer), worked through misreading the S1 multi-wafer numbering
(wafer 4, not wafer 1, carries the actual jack audio), and identified
an unlabeled schematic circle as a DIN jack only after finding the
physical rear-panel label, not from the schematic alone. Still not
live-tested at the bench.

Discharge-checked before opening up — no voltage on the caps.

Tagged pin 3's orange wire with a wrap of black tape before
desoldering, to distinguish it from pin 2's identical-looking orange
wire. Pin 2's orange wire is left untagged/plain.

Pulled the can, cut it open (Dremel, same method as C101/C102) and got
all three old paper elements out — a much tighter fit than either
single-section can, more residue to clean out. Base/terminal header
cleaned and confirmed against the earlier pinout work.

[[file:../photos/2026-09-11-c100-pinout-label-01.jpg]]

Test-fit the three discrete replacements (47µF, 22µF, 22µF) in the
can — just barely fits. This job needs extension wires (to common the
three sections' negatives into pin 4 and route three separate positive
leads out through the base) that C101/C102 didn't need, plus
insulation on those extensions given how little clearance there is.
The can's original internal wire covering looked like woven/braided
sleeving, not heat-shrink — not worth matching since none of this is
visible once the can's closed up.

Checked on hand: no hookup wire at all. Cheap generic "hookup wire
kit" options were mostly rated 300V insulation, marginal against this
node's actual spec voltage (270-296V, and likely higher yet at full
line) — decided against relying on taping over 300V wire for a
connection that gets sealed inside a can for years, rather than using
correctly-rated wire from the start. Ordered a 100ft spool of 22 AWG
red wire (arriving Sunday) rather than a pricier 6-color kit (arrives
Thursday) — voltage rating still needs to be confirmed against the
actual product on arrival, not assumed. Single color accepted as a
cost tradeoff; tagging leads by hand (as done for pin 3 tonight) is
the fallback for keeping track of which lead is which.

C100 is now fully disassembled — three new caps, the can, the clamp,
and the cleaned base sitting apart — and can't go back together until
the wire arrives. Decided to pivot to the 13 received oil-paper caps
in the meantime rather than sit idle, with a testing plan adapted to
what these caps actually need: verify each replacement's value and
check for shorts out-of-circuit before installing (rather than a live
power-up per cap, which doesn't tell you much about a coupling cap
anyway and isn't possible with C100 out), then treat the first
power-up after C100 goes back in as one comprehensive confirmation
sweep, run the same way as 2026-09-08's template.
