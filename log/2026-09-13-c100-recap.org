** C99

Considered just powering up with C99 still original now that C100 was
done, but decided against it and did C99 too rather than power up
partially recapped. Discharge-checked before starting.

*Physical wiring, confirmed before touching anything:* pin 1 = single
orange wire (to R114 or R115), tagged with black tape before
desoldering; pin 2 = brown wire on a long separate run; pin 3 = two
orange wires together (both R114/R115 taps); pin 4 = common/ground.
Also found a 100Ω-ish-looking resistor (brown-black-?-silver)
soldered directly across pins 1 and 2, leads under 1/2" — genuinely
on the can, not just nearby.

*Third-section hunt:* like C100, only two of C99's three sections
(both 20µF, fed by R114/R115) are drawn in the power-supply schematic
tiles (G9-I11, G10-I12). Searched all 24 schematic tiles for the
missing 40µF/third section — found it in tile-E7-G9, zone F8: C99's
third section decouples a 205V node in the FM IF strip near V3, not
anywhere near R114/R115. Confirmed physically: a brown-black-gold-
silver resistor on V3 pin 1 (R37) reads continuity to C99 pin 2 —
independent physical confirmation of the schematic-based find.

Two false leads on the way, both the same "drawn nearby ≠ connected"
trap this project keeps running into: R38 sits right next to C99's
label on the schematic but isn't on the same net (R37 is); R81
(10k) sits next to S6 on the page but isn't actually part of that
switch's circuit — it just happens to be drawn there.

*The pin 1/2 bridging resistor:* color bands were hard to read (first
band faded/scratched, third band impossible to distinguish orange
from brown by eye). In-circuit DMM reading climbed steadily — a
capacitor-charging artifact (same effect already documented for
VR2), not a stable value. Isolated it by lifting one leg: **9.84kΩ**,
matching R81's 10k spec closely enough to confirm. So R81 turns out
to do double duty — one leg bridges C99 pin 1 (40µF+) straight to
pin 2 (the 205V node), and from that same pin 2 junction a separate
wire continues on to S6's low-filter switch circuit. Full pinout now
known: pin 1 = 40µF+, pin 2 = 20µF+ (205V, via R81 from pin 1 and via
R37 to V3), pin 3 = two 20µF+ sections via R114/R115, pin 4 = common.

Dim-bulb watch/smell pass clean, then straight to full mains (see
Open questions for the voltage sweep findings and what got resolved
vs. what's still open).

Also did some rudimentary AM/FM listening checks with speakers on —
informal spot-checks, not real alignment or a thorough tuner
commissioning pass (that's still out of scope per Phase 9 item 2).
In FM stereo: loudness, balance, bass, treble, high filter, and low
filter all audibly modified the output, though how well loudness and
the filters work was hard to judge by ear (corrected 2026-09-18 per

Confirmed the S3/TAPE MONITOR override theory that's been open since

another, flipping S3 swapped cleanly between them independent of S1's

came through clear — clean program, no distortion — but over
noticeable scratchy noise that varied in intensity. Somewhat less than
AUX (possibly source-dependent: Mac vs. Mani 2). Correction

said "very low noise floor," which was wrong. Full writeup in

genuine second line-level input, no DIN adapter needed).

Volume pot (VR2) still a little scratchy — already investigated at

would likely help if it's bothersome, not urgent.

** Tube tap test (microphonics), following up on the AM hiss/squeal

First pass: tapped every tube except V1/V2/V3 (shielded) with a
wooden paintbrush handle, music still playing, volume up. Heard
nothing — inconclusive, since a subtle result could have been masked
by the music.

Second pass, done properly: paused the music, volume up, tapped every
tube again including V1/V2/V3's shields. Results:
- Most tubes: no reaction.
- V1: a little noise, but not a ring — sounded like the tap itself
  being amplified rather than the tube resonating. Milder, different
  category than V3's result.
- **V3: definite ring** — the classic microphonic signature (loose
  internal elements resonating after the mechanical excitation, not
  just picking up the knock itself).

Traced V3's actual role before concluding anything (tile-C5-E7): V3
is a recovery-gain stage immediately after the volume control, both
channels in one envelope — top section fed from VR2 1/2, bottom
section from VR2 2/2, each via a 0.1µF coupling cap (C21/C22). Plate
(pin 1) feeds through R37 to the C99 pin-2 decoupling node traced
last night. Not the single most hum/noise-sensitive point in the
chain (that's still V1/V2, per existing circuit-notes.org), but a
real, consequential position — right after volume, right before the
driver stage amplifies whatever V3 puts out further.

No spare 12AX7 on hand to swap-test with (the 2026-09-09 V1/V3 test
swapped positions between two existing tubes, not a third spare).
Ordered one 2026-09-14: JJ ECC803s, Low Noise & Microphonics graded
($34.53 total, TubeDepot) — that grading option specifically matches
what's being tested for, chosen over Balanced Triodes/High Gain
add-ons which don't apply to a lone single-position swap. See
bom.org's Order log. Still just a strong candidate until the swap
confirms or rules it out.

* Open questions

Resolved during tonight's sweep, not carried forward: V1 Pin 3/socket
thread fully closed — the topside socket-contact test (tube removed,
flagged since 2026-08-05) was actually done and read 3kΩ, matching
R9's spec cleanly; combined with Pin 3 coming back to 0.574V (in
spec) at full power, there's no remaining open question on V1. The
0V reading under the dim bulb was just a cold/under-heated artifact,
not the long-standing fault reasserting itself.
V14 Pin 6 (the "-0.9V, strange" reading was AUX mode leaving the AM
section unpowered, confirmed sensible once in AM mode); C100 Pin 2
appearing higher than Pin 1 (my own analysis error — they're parallel
branches, not a series cascade); C101-/C102+ jumping to 181.9V (ratio
against C101+ still matches the healthy 2026-09-10 doubler symmetry
almost exactly, just scaled to a higher overall B+ level). Suspected
cold joints were reflowed and resolved. Full readings and reasoning
in [[file:../measurements/2026-09-13-voltages.org][2026-09-13 measurements]] (dim-bulb pass) and
[[file:../measurements/2026-09-13-voltages-full-power.org][2026-09-13 measurements, full power]].

Still genuinely open:
- V4 Pin 6 :: 197.6V against an 85V spec (+132.5%) at full power —
  not new, a documented worsening outlier since 2026-08-15 (105V →
  118.7V → 197.6V). The biggest standing item from tonight's sweep.
- V6-V9 Pin 3 (shared cathode via C600) :: swung from -98% under the
  dim bulb to +79.7% at full power — genuinely unexplained, not
  cold-tube warm-up (that would just mean low, not overshooting).
  Possibly related to the AM noise below, given physical/circuit
  adjacency to the output stage, but not confirmed.
- AM reception is very noisy — not just fuzz, squeals and hum. Hum
  plausibly ties to the two voltage anomalies above (B+ filtering in
  the same neighborhood); squeals are more likely RF oscillation or
  alignment, which the plan already puts out of scope (Phase 9 item 2,
  turntable-only use case). Unknown whether this is new or a
  pre-existing condition — no earlier "AM tested clean" baseline
  exists to compare against.
- Loudness / high / low filter :: all audibly change the sound, but
  how well they work was hard to judge by ear — worth a cleaner A/B
  check later.
- V12 Pin 6 read 53.4V while nominally in AUX mode, which the
  project's own notes say should read ~0V on AUX (same as V14). Low
  priority — likely just means that particular reading happened after
  the mode had already changed, not a real fault.
