Discharge-checked the power supply before opening it up.

** C98
Confirmed C98's location (zone C4) and pulled it — a simple
point-to-point joint on both leads, not a riveted can terminal, so no
structural risk lifting it. Measured 25.8µF out of circuit against
20uF nominal, within period electrolytic tolerance; replacing anyway
per Phase 5 item 3.

New Rubycon 22µF 450V measured 22.14µF before install. Polarity
checked against the original. The replacement's leads were too short
to span the original run, so extended both with the old cap's leads,
twisted and soldered, not just butt-jointed. Positive-side splice is
fully insulated and routed through the same twist-tie bundle the
original used. Negative/ground-side splice is not fully insulated,
but it isn't touching anything and every nearby lead is also ground,
so accepted as-is. Cap itself isn't mechanically supported — matches
how the original was mounted, unsupported on its leads.

** C600
No replacement had actually been ordered for this one — bom.org still
showed status =spec= and the restoration plan's "Order C600
replacement: 68uF 200WV" TODO was never closed. Considered two
options from parts on hand: two spare 47uF/50V caps (originally
bought for C53/C54, which turned out not to physically exist —
their function is believed folded into C600's shared network), or a
spare 100uF/350V Nichicon (leftover from the C101/C102 order, qty 3
bought, 2 used). Ruled out the 47uF/450V spares meant for C99's
still-pending rebuild — using those would leave C99 short.

Went with the 100uF/350V Nichicon: closer to C600's measured 91uF
than the plan's 68uF target, massive voltage headroom over the
~1V cathode-bias node (itself an unconfirmed dim-bulb-limited reading
from 2026-08-15 that still needs a full-B+ recheck), and simpler as
one part at the original two tie points rather than paralleling two
smaller caps. Physically test-fit against the tag-board mount before
committing — fits.

** C19, C20
Last of the 13-cap oil-paper list originally in view tonight: the two
loudness caps, found buried behind the volume switch, one terminal
easy to reach and the other trapped under it. Initially misjudged one
as electrolytic from the can shape alone; the printed "OIL...400WV"
label corrected that once photographed closely.

Traced the schematic (S4 loudness switch / VR2 volume section)
carefully before touching anything: C19/C20 actually bridge S4's own
midpoint and pin 2, not the volume pot element directly — confirmed
by a physical jumper wire tying the switch's midpoint to the volume
pot's midpoint, which is why the old (larger) caps could physically
land at either point and still be correct. Landed the new, smaller
caps directly across the switch's midpoint and pin 2 instead of
running the longer original span to the volume pot — shorter lead
run enabled by the replacement's smaller size, not a topology change.

Access solution: clipped both old caps' leads a few mm out from the
body rather than trying to desolder the buried joint, then soldered
the new caps to those stubs. Original joints at both ends never
disturbed.

Couldn't tell which physical position was C19 vs C20 — mirrored pair,
doesn't matter functionally.

** C13, C14

cap body rather than desoldering either buried joint, and used the
resulting full-length original leads as extensions to solder the new
caps in. Both original joints undisturbed. 7 for 7 on oil-paper
failures at this point.
