* Work

**Oil paper coupling caps.** Oil paper goes leaky with age. A leaky
coupling cap puts positive DC on an output tube grid, drives it into
heavy conduction, and red-plates it — the classic vintage-tube
failure, and it can take out the output transformer. Modern E12
substitutes: 0.022 and 0.047 replace the original 0.02 and 0.05, well
within the original 10% tolerance. Original rating was 400 V; 630 V
costs pennies more and gives real margin. Went with Wima MKS4
(polyester) over MKP4 (polypropylene) — polypropylene would've been
the audiophile choice, but polyester is smaller, cheaper, and entirely
adequate here.

**Electrolytics.** Buy 450 V rather than 350 V on the B+ rails because
of the silicon-rectifier startup surge — this set uses SD-1A silicon
diodes for B+ rectification, not a tube rectifier, so there's no
soft-start. Nichicon/Panasonic 105°C parts. Restraint: don't inflate
the reservoir capacitance — later filter stages can go up somewhat,
but a much larger first cap increases rectifier surge current and
nudges B+ higher, so stayed close to original there. C502 and C506
are in the MPX section, which isn't in use, but a shorted electrolytic
there would still drag a shared rail — cheap insurance to replace them
anyway.

**C99/C100.** 40+20+20 µF at 350 V multi-section cans; Mouser doesn't
sell drop-in replacements. Compared four routes: Hayseed Hamfest
drop-in (default tube-community answer), CE Manufacturing authentic
twist-lock cans (period-correct look), restuffing the originals (most
authentic, most work), or discrete caps underneath with the original
can left in place cosmetically (easiest/cheapest, nothing shows from
outside). Landed on discrete — decided 2026-07-27, see
[[file:../er-420-restoration-plan.org][restoration-plan.org]] Phase 4.

**What's staying original.** Mica, ceramic disc, polyethylene, and
stirol (polystyrene) caps essentially don't fail with age — that
covers most of C29–C97 and nearly the whole MPX and front-end range,
so none of those get replaced. The polystyrene parts in the MPX
section are frequency-determining; swapping them would change an
alignment with no way to redo it, so hands off regardless of whether
the MPX section ever gets used. Mylar (C9, C10, C21, C22, C49–C52) is
generally reliable — test rather than replace outright.

Order placed with Mouser (281232819, $79.42) covering the above plus
fuses. Designator-level detail lives in [[file:../bom.org][bom.org]] now, not here.
