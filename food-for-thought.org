Outside perspective — forum advice, other people's experience, things
worth weighing that aren't a measurement or a part spec. Not a task
list: entries here inform decisions, they don't track them. Date
each one, note the source, and where it actually lands (agree,
disagree, still open) so this doesn't just become a pile of quotes
nobody revisits.

* Test oil-paper caps individually rather than blanket-replacing

Source: Dandy, Audiokarma reply, 2026-07-29. "Yes, the PIO caps can
be leaky, but all aren't. And the old electrolytics can also be good
after many years. Try not to change too much till you are confident
you know what really needs repair."

Status: open, unresolved against an existing decision. bom.org
currently has all 14 oil-paper designators (C7, C8, C13, C14, C19,
C20, C25, C26, C47, C48, C56, C60, C70, C96) marked for blanket
replacement, on the theory that oil-impregnated paper is a
known-bad-by-construction failure mode (see
[[file:log/2026-07-25-parts-research.org][parts-research.org]]) — not
a "maybe" the way old electrolytics are. Dandy's challenge is fair:
testing in-circuit first (the way Phase 2 already plans to test the
Mylars) would catch which ones are actually still fine, at the cost
of more bench time and more handling of 60-year-old solder joints per
cap kept in. Worth a deliberate decision either way, not a default.

* Restoration can strip a unit's "magic"

Source: Dandy, Audiokarma reply, 2026-07-29. Restored a Pioneer SX-82
fully and never liked the result; picked up a Pioneer SA-400 amp
needing no work at all and liked its sound as-is.

Status: noted, not actionable. Unmeasurable and subjective, but worth
remembering as a reason not to fix something that isn't actually
broken just because it's original and old — a check against
over-restoring corners of the set that already work fine.

* Long-term component ID tracking isn't standard practice

Source: gadget73, Audiokarma reply, 2026-07-31, asked directly how he
tracks which physical component is which while working. "Personally I
don't usually bother but if i have to order a bunch of parts, I will
use Mousers customer part number field to ID the new parts, then put
the old in the bag with that number. If there are 6 identical caps I
don't label each one though. If I'm working out of my parts stock, bad
parts just go into a bag or other container and once I'm done they go
in the trash."

Status: agree, and already the de facto practice here without anyone
stating it as a rule. He's answering from an experienced-tech,
fast-turnaround context — components are usually interchangeable once
identified, so nothing to track long-term. This project's reason for
[[file:component-notes.org][component-notes.org]] is different (beginner, first-time
verification work, don't want to redo it after walking away for
months) but the scoping instinct matches exactly: every component that
got the deep-dive treatment tonight (R79/R80, C13/C14, C96, T7/T8) had
a real question attached, not just "label it because it exists" — the
four identical C49-C52 Mylars got confirmed-same-spec-and-clean, not
individually distinguished. Worth adopting directly: the Mouser
customer-PN-field + bagged-old-part trick, once Phase 6 pulling parts
actually starts, as a cheap physical complement to the digital
tracking rather than a replacement for it.
